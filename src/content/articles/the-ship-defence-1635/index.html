The Ship Defence, 1635

An annotated and searchable passenger list from the Great Puritan Migration

London to Boston · Arrival reported October 8, 1635 · Source: Charles Edward Banks, The Planters of the Commonwealth, pp. 167–170

In the summer of 1635, the Defence of London, commanded by Edward Bostock, sailed for the Massachusetts Bay Colony. Charles Edward Banks reported that the ship departed about the end of July and reached Boston on October 8 with approximately one hundred passengers.

Among the names in Banks’s compilation are Jasper Gunn, 29; Mrs. Anne Gunn, 25; and Phebe Maulder, 7. Their listing is an important lead in reconstructing Jasper Gunn’s migration and in investigating the identity of his wife.

About this edition. This is a searchable adaptation of Ken Gunn’s June 3, 1996 transcription of Banks’s published compilation, not a fresh transcription of an original passenger register. The two passenger categories below are kept separate, and ambiguities have not been silently resolved.

The Gunn entry

As transcribed, the Gunn party consists of Jasper Gunn (29), Mrs. Anne Gunn (25), and Phebe Maulder (7). The destination “Milford, CT” attached to Jasper in the compilation reflects a later association, not the ship’s Boston port of arrival.

The entry for Anne is consistent with—but does not prove a connection to—the indexed January 14, 1633 marriage of Jasper Gun and Anne Legge at Sandon, Essex. Other documents refer to Jasper’s wife or widow as Mary or Christian. Phebe Maulder’s relationship, if any, to the Gunns remains unestablished. These distinctions matter in the research presented in The Gunns in America.

Transcription cautions

The original supplied text repeats an Elizabeth French entry with conflicting ages (32 and 30); both appearances are preserved. One servant in the Harlakenden party is identified only as “Robert......” in the supplied text, represented here as “Robert” with an incomplete-name note. Several fractional ages are retained as written. Banks’s place labels may refer to subsequent destinations rather than places of embarkation.

This edition preserves reported spellings and ages without asserting that the source is an exhaustive list of everyone who traveled. For proof of a particular person’s migration, examine the underlying record and corroborating contemporary evidence wherever available.
