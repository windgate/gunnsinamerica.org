A physician's chest, a great Bible, an orchard, and a room reserved for his wife: Jasper Gunn's will brings us close to the daily life of his household. Written on Christmas Day in 1670, it distributes his property among his children and sets out the care his widow was to receive for the rest of her life.

The provisions are practical and personal. Abel was to receive Jasper's medical books and the chest containing his medicines and surgical equipment. Daniel and Samuel would divide much of the land, livestock, and working equipment, while Samuel would inherit the house. With that inheritance came obligations: the brothers were to provide their mother with suitable food, medicine, and whatever else she needed, "with all love & quietnes."

For the history of Jasper's wife, the document is significant but limited. He calls her his "loving wife" and "their mother," but does not name her. This will therefore cannot, by itself, resolve the question of Ann, Mary, and Christian.

*The text below follows Ken Gunn's transcription of July 1, 1996, supplied for this article. Paragraphing, capitalization, and punctuation have been adjusted for readability; common abbreviations have been expanded and some spelling modernized. Uncertain readings are identified in brackets. This is a reading edition, not a transcription checked against the original manuscript.*

> The last will and testament of Jasper Gun of Milford in the County of New Haven.
>
> First, I give to my son Jobamah a piece of land in the new field. It is about 40 rod long, and from his side line now brake up to the common fences. I suppose ten acres, more or less.
>
> **Item.** I give to my son Abell Gun ten pounds, to be paid by my executors within five years after my decease. Also, I give unto Abell my physic chest, with all physic and chirurgery things in it. Also, I give unto Abell all my physic books. In case the said Abel Gun dies without heirs, my will is that my books and [chest; transcribed as "check"] be returned back to the rest of my sons. Also, I give unto Abel my best cloth cloak, and the divinity books he hath now of mine, and one of Mr. Hooker's.
>
> **Item.** I give unto Daniell Gun, my son, half all my lands and meadows, except Dreadfull Lott and my home lot. Only my will is that Daniell shall have a piece of my home lot two rods wider than his brother Jobamah's orchard is, all the length of my home lot from Jobamah's orchard to the further end.
>
> **Item.** I give to my son Samuel my house, housing, orchard, gardens, and Dreadfull Lott, with half my other lands and meadows, equally divided between Daniell and Samuel. Also, I give unto Samuel my great Bible and fowling piece; and after his mother's decease, I give unto Samuel my best feather bed, with all that belongs to it.
>
> **Item.** I give to my sons Daniell and Samuel all my cattle, horses, cart, and all implements belonging thereunto, with my tools of all sorts. I say, to be equally divided between them two.
>
> Further, my will is that when them two, Daniell and Samuel, shall part one from another to dwell by themselves, I say my will is that Samuel join with Daniell in labour and charge to build him such housings as I now have.
>
> **Item.** My will is that my loving wife, their mother, shall have her dwelling in the house, and have the parlor at her dispose during her life, and abiding in the house as she shall [illegible word].
>
> **Item.** My will is that Daniell and Samuel, my sons, shall from time to time, with all love and quietness, provide all suitable food and physic, and what else she needs, for her lifetime.
>
> **Item.** My will is, if Daniell or Samuel die without heirs, that the estate of either of them shall return to the survivor or survivors of my own body.
>
> **Item.** My will is that Daniell and Samuel, my sons, shall be my sole executors.
>
> **Item.** I give to my daughter Mehetabell Fenn one of Mr. Hooker's books and Mr. Aynsworth's *Communion of Saints*.
>
> My desire is that my loving brothers, John Smith and Joseph Pecke, shall be overseers of this my last will.
>
> If my wife please, she shall have twenty bushels of corn paid to her yearly by my executors; that is, five of wheat, five rye, five peas, five Indian.
>
> Signed and confirmed with mine own hand this December 25, 1670.
>
> **Jasper Gun**
>
> **Witnesses:** John Smith and Joseph Pecke.

## Probate and inventory

The supplied transcription records that the witnesses were sworn in court on **January 27, 1670**. That date is retained here as transcribed. It may reflect the old-style calendar year, in which January followed December without a change in the recorded year; if so, it would be January 27, 1670/71. The original probate entry should be checked before adopting that interpretation as certain.

The inventory is recorded as **288.0.10**, conventionally read as **£288, 0 shillings, and 10 pence**.

The names following the inventory in the supplied transcription are Benjamin Fenn, George Clarke, and Richard Platt. The word "appraisers" follows George Clarke's name; the precise arrangement and roles should be confirmed from the manuscript.

## What the will preserves

Jasper's bequests place medical practice, farming, and religious reading within the same household. The physic chest and surgical equipment document the tools of his work; the cattle, cart, and land show the material foundation of family life. His books were possessions worth assigning by name or subject, passing to both a son and a daughter.

His widow's care was written into the transfer of that property. She was to retain a place in the house and control of the parlor. Daniel and Samuel were charged with her support, and she could choose an annual allowance of grain. Yet the woman at the center of these provisions remains unnamed—a reminder of how much a surviving document can reveal, and how much it can leave unanswered.

## Editorial notes

- **Physic and chirurgery:** Medicine and surgery; the bequest includes medicines and surgical equipment.
- **"Now brake up":** Retained from the supplied transcription. The wording of this land boundary needs comparison with the manuscript.
- **"Check":** The supplied text reads "books & check." "Chest" is the probable intended reading, but remains an editorial suggestion.
- **Dreadfull Lott:** Retained as the name of a parcel; the will supplies no explanation of the name.
- **"Brothers":** Jasper's description of John Smith and Joseph Pecke is preserved. The term alone does not establish whether the relationship was by blood, marriage, or religious fellowship.
- **Corn:** The will uses the word collectively for grain, specifying wheat, rye, peas, and "Indian," meaning Indian corn or maize.
- **Mr. Hooker and Mr. Aynsworth:** The names and book references follow the supplied text. No further identification or edition is asserted here.
