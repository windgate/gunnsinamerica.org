Among the passengers of the *Defence* in the summer of 1635, the ship's manifest lists a "John" Shepard, with his wife, Margaret, and their infant son, Thomas.[^1] The first name was probably an alias. The man was Thomas Shepard, a Puritan minister who had been silenced for nonconformity, and ministers in his position had reason to travel discreetly. The same list records [Jasper Gunn](/people/jasper-gunn), aged twenty-nine, and Ann Gunn, aged twenty-five, under a variant spelling of their surname.[^2]

## The Witness

Shepard was born about 1604 in Northamptonshire and studied at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, where he took his B.A. and M.A. He was ordained in 1627. Three years later William Laud, Bishop of London, silenced him as a lecturer. After his emigration he became pastor of the First Church of Newtown, now Cambridge, Massachusetts.[^3]

Late in life Shepard wrote the earliest surviving colonial autobiography. He composed it after 1647 for his son Thomas, and it was not published until 1832.[^4] It contains the only known account of the *Defence*'s crossing. The Gunns shared the ship, so Shepard's pages are the nearest we can come to their experience of the voyage.

Shepard thought poorly of the vessel. "The ship we came in was very rotten and unfit for such a voyage," he wrote, "and therefore the first Storme we had, we had a very great leaker, which did much appeal and affect us."[^5]

He read the trouble as providence. God, he believed, had shown the company its unfitness, and the unfitness of their earlier attempt, which a storm at sea had cut short the year before.[^6]

In another gale the ship's violent motion threw Margaret Shepard against an iron bolt as she held their infant son. The child was unharmed, and she recovered. The chill she took on the passage, Shepard recorded, later developed into the consumption that killed her.[^7]

## Landfall

The *Defence* reached Boston on 3 October 1635.[^8] Margaret Shepard died about five months after the family's arrival. In 1637 Shepard married Joanna Hooker, daughter of the Reverend Thomas Hooker.[^9]

## Jasper and Ann

Jasper and Ann Gunn had married two years before the crossing, in 1633, at Sandon, where she had been Ann Legge.[^10] They were a young couple, newly arrived.

Ann appears on the manifest and in no later record.[^11] Whether she survived the voyage is unknown. One possibility, supported by no document, is that she died aboard or soon after landing, as Margaret Shepard did within months. That is speculation, and nothing in the record confirms it.

Jasper settled in Roxbury. His first son, [Nathaniel](/people/nathaniel-gunn-1640), was born there between about 1637 and 1640.[^12] A later wife, Christian, is documented in her will of 1690.[^13] How many wives Jasper had remains an open question, taken up in [Christian, Mary, or Anne?](/articles/christian-mary-or-anne)
