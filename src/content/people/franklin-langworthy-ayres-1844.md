**Died:** 1926
**Father:** [Burton Ayres](/people/burton-ayres-1805/) (1805–1870)

## A Son of the Illinois Frontier

Franklin Langworthy Ayres was born in 1844 along the Illinois River, where his father had arrived while northern Illinois was still being opened to Euro-American settlement. Local histories name Burton as the first white settler of La Salle Township. Franklin's mother, Aurelia Langworthy, appears far less often in those accounts, though sustaining a household in a new settlement required considerable labor. Her family name survives in his middle name.

Franklin was about four years old when the Illinois and Michigan Canal opened in 1848. La Salle grew from a small settlement into a canal, rail, and commercial center during his childhood.

## Family, Education, and Early Work

Burton was a blacksmith, a trade on which every frontier community depended for tools, iron fittings, and farm repairs. No evidence indicates that Franklin followed him into the trade. His schooling and his occupation before leaving Illinois are not documented in the surviving record.

## The Civil War Years

Franklin was sixteen or seventeen when the war began in April 1861. No military service record has been identified for him, and whether he served remains an open question.

## Marriage to Elizabeth Sarah Gunn

Franklin married [Elizabeth Sarah “Lizzie” Gunn](/people/elizabeth-sarah-gunn-ayres-1846/) on 5 January 1868 at the First Baptist Church in La Salle. Elizabeth belonged to the Gunn family, whose line runs from colonial New England into the Midwest.

The marriage is documented in a fiftieth-anniversary family record dated 5 January 1918, preserved in the *MHGS Register* of the Midwest Historical and Genealogical Society.[^1] The record names the church, the couple's Kansas residences, and seven children, and it preserves how the family itself remembered its history.

## The Journey West

Franklin left La Salle in the autumn of 1871 and traveled to Kansas ahead of his family. A later newspaper recollection attributed to him states:

> "I left La Salle in the fall of 1871… went west alone, came back in 1872 and moved my family…"[^3]

The sequence was a common one: the husband scouted land or work, then returned for his wife and children. Kansas had been a state only since 1861, and railroads, land sales, and farm settlement were reshaping it.

The anniversary record ties the family to Indianola and Augusta, Kansas. Augusta, in Butler County, lies on the Walnut River east of Wichita and was then developing as an agricultural and trading center. For Franklin it was a second frontier. His father had helped settle northern Illinois; Franklin now brought his own family into a region changing in much the same way.

Kansas was not empty land. Native nations had long histories there, and the settlement that drew families like the Ayreses came amid federal policies that displaced them.

## Building a Family in Kansas

The anniversary record names seven children:

- Charlotte "Lottie" Ayres, who married Merritt A. Nelson
- Stella Elizabeth Ayres, who married Anderson Holladay
- Warren Elliott Ayres, who married Susie Fearhake
- Melton Durley Ayres (1879–1952)
- Jessie Evelyn Ayres, who married Franklin Corbin
- Franklin Burton Ayres, named for his paternal grandfather
- Stanley Edwin Ayres (1891–1969)

Other family information adds Allan Brainard Ayres (1868–1877), who died as a child. He does not appear in the anniversary record, and his place in the family awaits confirmation against the family Bible and census records.[^5] Lottie reportedly became a teacher.

Franklin and Elizabeth lived to see their children grown and a new generation begin.

## Work and Community Life

Franklin remained connected to Kansas for more than fifty years, from the early 1870s until his death. United States census schedules list his occupation as farmer in the enumerations before 1910. In the 1910 census, when he was about sixty-five, his occupation appears as mail carrier.[^7] The census entries do not specify whether the route was rural or in town.

During those decades Augusta and Butler County changed substantially. Railroads widened regional markets, farming became more specialized, and commercial institutions grew with the population.[^6]

## Nina Gunn and the 1900 Census

The 1900 census, enumerated at Bruno in Butler County, records the Ayres household with a boarder, [Nina Gunn](/people/nina-belle-gunn-pyle/). She was Elizabeth's niece, the daughter of Elizabeth's brother Aaron Elihu Gunn, and she was living with her aunt and uncle as she began her career as a teacher.[^8]

In the same month as the enumeration, Nina married William Pyle of Towanda. The ceremony took place at the home of Franklin and Elizabeth ("Lizzie").[^9] The household thus sheltered Nina at the start of her working life and hosted her marriage, tying the Ayres family directly to the Gunn and Pyle lines that follow.

## Remembering La Salle

Later in life Franklin returned to La Salle, and a newspaper account preserved his recollections of leaving in 1871 and coming back the next year for his family. By then the town had become an established industrial and transportation center. As Burton's son, Franklin could speak from memory about the settlement's earliest years, at a time when communities were gathering pioneer recollections through newspaper interviews and county histories.

## Fifty Years of Marriage

Franklin and Elizabeth marked their golden anniversary on 5 January 1918, with the country at war and the automobile coming into common use. Their marriage had begun in La Salle three years after the Civil War. The anniversary record gives the full names of both spouses, the church, and all seven children, making it the principal link between the Illinois and Kansas branches of the family.

## Final Years and Death

Elizabeth died in 1923, after about fifty-five years of marriage. Franklin died in 1926, at about eighty-two. Born before the Mexican-American War, he lived through the Civil War, the settlement of Kansas, the railroad age, and the First World War.

## Legacy

Burton Ayres helped establish a settlement. Franklin remembered its beginnings and carried that history west. His marriage to Elizabeth Sarah Gunn joined the Ayres line to the Gunns, and through their children the two families continued across the Midwest and beyond. In *The Gunns in America*, Franklin represents the generation that inherited a frontier town, watched it mature, and then took the work of settlement farther west.
