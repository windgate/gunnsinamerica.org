Christian Gunn was Jasper Gunn's wife when he died at Milford in January 1670/71, and she outlived him by almost twenty years. Two wills establish that much. His provides for his "loving wife, their mother," and never gives her name. Hers, made on 6 May 1690, names her Christian Gunn and calls her his widow.

The rest of her story is harder to fix. No record found so far says when she married Jasper, where she was born, or which of his children she bore. This account sets out what the records say and keeps what later writers have assumed apart from it.

## The Record at a Glance

| Date | Record | What it shows about Jasper's wife |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14 Jan 1633 | Marriage of Jasper Gun and Anne Legge, Sandon, Essex[^1] | Jasper married a woman named Anne before he sailed |
| 1635 | Passenger list of the *Defence*[^2] | Jasper sailed with Anne Gunn, surname recorded as Goun, taken here to be Anne Legge |
| 25 Apr 1641 | Milford church admission, reported by Anderson[^3] | Jasper and his wife Mary admitted, dismissed from the Hartford church |
| 25 Dec 1670 | Will of Jasper Gun[^4] | "My loving wife, their mother," unnamed |
| 6 May 1690 | Will of Christian Gunn, proved 1 Dec 1690[^5] | Christian, widow of Jasper |

Three names appear in these records: Anne, Mary, and Christian. Only one, Christian, is attached to Jasper's widow. The others belong to women recorded as his wife earlier.

## What Jasper's Will Says About Her

Jasper wrote his will on Christmas Day 1670. He names five children: Jobamah, Abel, Daniel, Samuel, and his daughter Mehetabell Fenn. Of his wife he writes only that she is his "loving wife, their mother."

The provisions for her are specific. She was to live in the house and have "the parlor at her dispose" for the rest of her life. Daniel and Samuel were to give her food, medicine, and whatever else she needed, "with all love & quietnes." If she chose, she could take twenty bushels of corn a year, five each of wheat, rye, peas, and Indian corn. After her death Samuel was to have the best feather bed.[^4]

The phrase "their mother" sits in the clauses about Daniel and Samuel. It follows the provisions that divide Jasper's land and goods between those two, and the clause after it makes them responsible for her care. The natural reading is that "their" means Daniel and Samuel. The will does not say that she was also the mother of Jobamah, Abel, Mehitable, or the older Nathaniel, who had died before 1670.

Christian's will names her as Jasper's widow, so she was the wife he left. That makes her the "their mother" of Daniel, baptized at Milford in September 1645, and of Samuel, born about 1648. The text we have is a reading edition. The original probate record has not yet been compared with it.

## Her Own Will

Christian made her will on 6 May 1690. It was proved on 1 December 1690, and her inventory was taken on 31 October.[^5] Her death therefore fell in 1690, after the will and before the inventory. She names her daughter "Mehetabell Campe" and remembers her Fenn grandchildren, leaving Bibles to her grandsons Benjamin and Joseph Fenn.

Susan Woodruff Abbott reports that Christian's will names all of the same children that Jasper named in his.[^6] Her summary needs checking against the will itself. By 1690 Abel (died 1688) and Nathaniel (died before 1670) were dead, and Daniel probably was too, so "the same children" cannot mean the same living children. How the will treats them can only be read from its text. A transcription of the will is not yet on this site.

## Three Names, One Widow

The records give Jasper three women: Anne, Mary, and Christian.

**Anne.** This account treats Anne Legge of Sandon and Anne Gunn (recorded Goun) of the *Defence* as one woman, and as Jasper's first known wife. Three points support the identification. The names match, and so do the county and the dates. The ages fit: Jasper was baptized at Great Burstead, Essex, in 1607, and the passenger list puts him at twenty-nine in 1635 and her at twenty-five. That suggests a woman born about 1610 who married at about twenty-three. And no other Jasper Gun or Gunn has been found in Essex or the neighboring counties, so the 1607 baptism, the 1633 marriage, and the 1635 passenger are taken to be one man.[^8]

The identification is a working conclusion, not a record. Each link in the chain depends on how rare the name is, and no single document joins the baptism, the marriage, and the voyage. It does mean that the Anne on the passenger list was a real woman named Anne, not a clerk's error for Christian.

No later record mentions Anne. This account assumes she died during the crossing or soon after reaching New England. That is an assumption. No death or burial record has been found, and in this period the lack of one proves little in either direction.

**Mary and Christian.** Anderson lists Jasper's wife as Christian, surname unknown, and places her marriage to Jasper by about 1637.[^3] No record examined for this account that was made before 1690 calls her Christian. The name appears in her own will. It continued in the family: Jobamah's daughter Christian Elizabeth (1677–1757) and Samuel's daughter Christian (born about 1695) both carry it, and the family pages say they were named for their grandmother. Naming a child for a grandmother is common and does not prove anything about her earlier life. It does show that "Christian" was a name used in the family while she lived.

If Anne was Jasper's first wife, Christian was a later one, and two readings remain open for Mary:

- Mary and Christian were one woman, recorded under different names.
- Mary was a separate wife who died, and Christian followed her.

The simplest reading has one wife from 1641 until 1670. The records are consistent with that, but they do not require it. "Mary" and "Christian" are separate given names, and no record shows one used for the other. Joining them as "Mary Christian" would hide the question without answering it.

## Where She Came From

Her maiden name is not recorded, and this account does not supply one. Later genealogies call her a Baldwin of Cholesbury, Buckinghamshire. That is conjecture. No record calls Jasper's wife a Baldwin, and no record connects her to any family before her marriage.

Some circumstances make the idea plausible. A Richard Baldwin of Cholesbury left a will naming a daughter Christian.[^7] A baptism of a Christian Baldwin there on 17 January 1615/16 has been reported, and a woman baptized then would have been about twenty-one at a marriage in 1637. Three Baldwin brothers from Cholesbury, Timothy, Nathaniel, and Joseph, are said to have lived in the same New England towns as the Gunns.

None of this is evidence that Jasper's wife was a Baldwin. It consists of a shared first name, a workable age, and relatives nearby. The fit is convenient, and convenience is a reason for caution. One detail does not fit easily: the route proposed for her runs through the *Martin*, which reached New England in 1638, later than Anderson's estimate of a marriage by about 1637.

For these reasons the site gives her no maiden name. She is Christian Gunn, maiden name unknown. A record would settle it: a baptism or marriage entry naming her, or a will calling her Jasper Gunn's wife and naming her family.

Dates follow each child's own page. Some accounts place Nathaniel's birth at Roxbury in 1637 or 1638, which would make him the eldest.

Christian is usually assumed to have been the mother of all six. She may have been. Neither will, as examined here, says so for the four older children. The question matters most for Jobamah and Nathaniel, who were born before the Milford record of Mary in 1641, and for Mehitable, baptized that May.

## Research Notes

1. **Christian's will.** Obtain the probate record. Transcribe the clauses on her children, the treatment of Abel, Nathaniel, and Daniel, and the executors. Confirm the dates of making, inventory, and probate.
2. **Jasper's will.** Compare the reading edition with the original in New Haven Probate Records, vol. 1, part 2, pp. 39–40. Confirm that "their mother" follows the Daniel and Samuel clauses.
3. **Milford church entry, 25 April 1641.** Confirm Anderson's wording, and whether the entry says "Mary" or something else. Pages on this site disagree on whether any record in her lifetime names Mary.
4. **Passenger list.** Check the spelling, ages, and order of Jasper's party against Hotten's printed text and the original. Site pages give Goun and Gonn, Ann, Anne, and Anna.
5. **Sandon marriage.** Add the parish-register reference. Confirm that the Jasper Gun who married Anne Legge is the Jasper who sailed.
6. **Residences.** Pages on this site give different dates for Jasper's years at Roxbury, Hartford, and Milford. Take the sequence from Anderson.
7. **Baldwin conjecture.** To test it, confirm the date of Richard Baldwin's will, find the parish-register entry for the baptism, and establish which Richard and Isabelle Baldwin of Buckinghamshire are meant. Until a record names her family, Baldwin is not used as her maiden name.
8. **Jasper's identity.** Record where and how other Jasper Gun, Gunn, Goun, and Gonn entries were searched for in Essex and the neighboring counties: the databases, the spellings, and the date range. The identification of the 1607 baptism, the 1633 marriage, and the 1635 passenger rests on that search.
9. **Anne's fate.** Look for a burial or death entry for an Anne Gunn or Goun at sea, at Boston, or at Roxbury between 1635 and 1641. Look also for what became of Phoebe Maulder.
